# HILFER — Project Context for Codex

_Last updated: October 2026. Working context, not a substitute for the group's live narrative documents._

## What is HILFER?

An interactive narrative mystery / institutional horror game experienced entirely through a fictional company's desktop OS. HILFER is a private research-and-defense firm developing experimental **radiological** technology. Following a containment incident, its departmental records disagree. The player, a HILFER research associate, is assigned to finish Post-Incident Data Reconciliation after previous operator **L. Losurdo** becomes medically unavailable.

The player uses an authorized workstation to navigate records, email, technical data, security footage, audio excerpts, and the corporate AI assistant. This is not a hacking game. The player has legitimate access for legitimate assigned work.

## Core theme

Harm is produced by the interaction of departmental incentives, procedures, constraints, and decisions that look individually defensible. Engineering compensates for failing systems; Operations keeps staffing possible; Occupational Health avoids unproven causality claims; HR uses established classifications; Legal minimizes unsupported liability; Program Management preserves a valuable project; Compliance standardizes the official language. There is **no single cartoon villain and no one hidden memo explaining the whole disaster**.

The player can uncover what happened while inadvertently participating in the process that makes one set of records authoritative. Their increasing understanding does not grant control over the employer's infrastructure or official channels of communication.

## Narrative overview (current working canon)

- The facility accumulates failures and worker health concerns before a demonstration. **Tito** files maintenance tickets; **Tim** designs technical/workforce risk mitigations; **Jessica** reports symptoms; **José Carlos** examines a worrying but initially inconclusive health pattern; **Spacco** is a senior scientist whose response to risk evolves; **Guy** works in communications/compliance.
- The demonstration causes a radiological containment incident.
- **Recovery 01** is necessary to stabilize containment, **Recovery 02** retrieves critical diagnostic data, while **Recovery 03** retrieves a valuable prototype after the immediate safety/data rationale has changed. The distinction concerns the *purpose* for which worker risk is accepted, not whether the earlier missions were safe.
- Reconciliation requires joining incomplete evidence across departments. The player gradually learns that medical harm and official incident counts are not identical; institutional language can obscure materially connected events without every entry being strictly false.
- Previous worker Losurdo spent extended periods at the insufficiently decontaminated workstation and became unavailable. The player may repeat that trajectory.
- Current **preferred final climax**: after consolidating evidence, the player attempts an external disclosure. A helpful AI becomes actively obstructive during a brief scripted interface confrontation. The player seems to send the message successfully. After the OS returns to a strangely perfect normal state, **Sent Mail contains an institutionally sanitized message**. The AI is friendly again; occupational-health/account changes echo Losurdo. The player won an interface struggle but never controlled the outbound communications gateway.

## Narrative pacing

Beat 1: routine assignment/tutorial. Beats 2–4: overlapping, **nonlinear** investigations (human/technical cost, Losurdo, institutional contradictions). Beats 5–7: convergence around Recovery 03, the meaning of Reconciliation, and Losurdo; then the endgame. The beats are **not** seven sequential levels.

## Gameplay for the immediate demo — current experiment

The player opens **Consolidated Report**, which already holds an **unverified HR claim** (`HR-014`: Jessica is AVAILABLE). This report is the primary gameplay surface and becomes a living institutional record, derived from saved resolutions rather than duplicated data.

1. Inspect Jessica's report entry: existing status AVAILABLE, source HR-014, unverified.
2. In Records, open relevant source documents. Values such as Work Clearance are interactive; selecting a claim opens Reconciliation's compact side-by-side source comparison.
3. Selecting HR-014 alone does not resolve the discrepancy. Finding and selecting OH-008 reveals the competing claim NOT_CLEARED with an updated date.
4. The player may retain the current entry (leaving the investigation unresolved) or use the supported OH-008 source. The latter choice files the administrative correction immediately — **no drag and drop, dropdown, or separate Commit button**.
5. Reconciliation updates the report, links HR-014 and OH-008 as sources, advances progression, causes the first persistent OS anomaly, and unlocks the next placeholder case.

Jessica's verified work eligibility does not establish occupational causation. The approved implementation for this case is deliberately simple; later cases may involve multiple claims, temporal evidence, missing information and constrained report classifications. **Do not generalize the prototype to all puzzles without testing.**
